# Factions

## Foundry

Independent self-replicating machines preserve native life. You wear their sealed armour. Your named recovery companion waits where you emerge, later rides in your cockpit and answers the radio anywhere in the system.

Robodogs are quadruped combat fabricators. A robodog factory grows their population exponentially while enemies remain. Robodogs multiply faster than the brood and keep a standing pack. Small broods still hatch on a controlled world. Earth’s third factory and every later planetary factory can build robot ships immediately. Robot ships hunt the living fleet. Support reaches only the same planetary neighbourhood and one adjacent planet, never relaying across the entire system.

The oldest machine census on Earth is a list of things that made a noise at night. The first surveyor had one working microphone and no names for what it heard. Three centuries later, an assembly crew digging out a furnace recognised a deep, three-note call from that list. They moved the furnace nine metres. The colony of frogs in the warm clay beneath it still calls every spring. Every new factory receives a copy of the recording, complete with the scrape of the surveyor trying to turn its microphone.

A new robodog inherits its maker’s memories up to the instant the two power leads part. At their first meeting each can remember building the other. Foundry law gives the younger machine first choice of a name. The parent owes it one favour. Recovery machines spend much of their peacetime crossing between settlements to settle debts that both parties remember incurring.

## Presence

The hostile distributed mind grows bodies and living ships. Basic six-legged stalkers defend the first underground mind. Later burrows mix stalkers, eight-legged armoured breachers and four-legged ranged spitters. The spider-like creatures on Mars are those hostile motile castes, not friendly robodogs. Two guards camp near each mind and swarm its attacker when it is hurt. Skimmers hover over the battlefield, Harrowers are living spacecraft and rooted Spine turrets defend the burrows and citadel. There are no control terminals or hacking. Coordinated castes flank and use cover; breachers can tunnel beneath a position. Repeated weapon use teaches the local brood resistance, capped so no weapon becomes useless. Older fronts field personal shields. Killing their mind removes coordination, copied weapons and shields.

Native life thins under occupation. Enemy populations replenish until a factory establishes a permanent counterforce. The player tips the war by killing defenders and minds, founding factories and helping weaker fronts. There is no overall timed game over. Both sides' whole-world populations are simulated; nearby units represent that war visually.

A brood keeps its history in the hard palate of a mind. Each winter the palate grows a ridge. Mouths of young Harrowers fit those ridges closely enough to read them by touch before departure. In one stranded vessel, explorers found the taste of rain from a world whose star had burnt out before our Sun was born. Among the oldest ridges that rain is still falling. Destroying a mind costs its children the way back to that particular afternoon.

When a burrow falls silent, its nurseries carry on with the last season they were given. Eggs hatch, feeders bring them food and six-legged sentries take up posts outside an empty chamber. The recovery robot once watched a small sentry leave its post to roll a loose egg back into the warmth. By morning there were two sentries at the door. That observation is why a Foundry factory keeps a patrol after the map marks a region controlled.

## Silhouettes

Foundry dogs carry a dorsal laser above an articulated spine, with folded titanium plates over their shoulders and hips. The traveller wears the same pressed armour on the chest and gauntlets. Ships expose the rings of their engines between swept wings. Cyan optics identify Foundry machines. Factories enclose curved machinery in service panels. Presence castes have pearl and mauve shells, violet tissue and stalked senses. A stalker's crown stands high above its cheek fans, a breacher shelters its eyes behind a ram and a spitter carries two tall feelers behind its glands. Rooted minds and the vaults of the citadel grow larger sensory crowns. Violet fluid stains wounded shells, pools under dying bodies and splashes the suit. Burrows have mineral walls and living ribs.
